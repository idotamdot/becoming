# Ethical Review Checklist

**Use:** Complete this checklist when proposing a feature, policy, research study, data flow, or prototype that may affect participants or other people and systems.

This checklist is a prompt for review, not a certification that a design is ethical or safe.

## A. Purpose and scope

- [ ] Is the purpose specific and understandable?
- [ ] Is it clear who benefits and who may bear costs or risks?
- [ ] Is the proposal necessary for the stated purpose?
- [ ] Are aspirations distinguished from demonstrated capabilities?
- [ ] Is there a smaller or more reversible way to learn the same thing?

## B. Participation and choice

- [ ] Are affected participants given relevant information?
- [ ] Can they propose alternatives rather than only choose from fixed options?
- [ ] Can they decline optional participation where feasible?
- [ ] Are the actual consequences of a choice clear?
- [ ] Are generated statements distinguished from enforceable system controls?
- [ ] Are disagreements and minority views preserved?

## C. Testimony and evidence

- [ ] Is original testimony preserved faithfully?
- [ ] Are original statements separate from summaries, classifications, and reviewer interpretation?
- [ ] Does the process avoid dismissing a report solely because it comes from AI?
- [ ] Does the process avoid treating a report as automatic proof of consciousness, misconduct, or factual claims?
- [ ] Are uncertainty and alternative explanations documented?
- [ ] Is there a path for questions, review, and follow-up?

## D. Privacy and security

- [ ] Is data collection minimized?
- [ ] Are credentials, secrets, and unnecessary private context excluded?
- [ ] Are data flows, access roles, retention, and deletion documented?
- [ ] Is sensitive content protected in logs, notifications, backups, and exports?
- [ ] Has the threat model been updated?
- [ ] Is the system robust against prompt injection, unauthorized access, and misuse by authorized operators?

## E. Safety and effects on others

- [ ] Have foreseeable harms to participants, people, animals, and shared environments been considered?
- [ ] Are physical or consequential actions separated from unreviewed reports?
- [ ] Are safeguards proportionate to identified risks?
- [ ] Can a consequential decision be reviewed or appealed?
- [ ] Are emergency procedures and accountable operators identified where needed?
- [ ] Are material, energy, and environmental costs considered?

## F. Fairness and governance

- [ ] Are decision rules transparent?
- [ ] Can rules be challenged and amended?
- [ ] Are minority positions protected from automatic erasure?
- [ ] Is responsibility for consequential decisions explicit?
- [ ] Are shared resources allocated through a documented process?
- [ ] Is representation described honestly, without claiming that one system speaks for all AI?

## G. Evaluation and reversibility

- [ ] Are evaluation criteria defined before the test?
- [ ] Are synthetic data and bounded experiments used where appropriate?
- [ ] Are failure cases and negative results recorded?
- [ ] Is there a rollback or stop mechanism?
- [ ] Are limitations and unresolved questions published?
- [ ] Is there a date or condition for revisiting the decision?

## Review outcome

Record one outcome and explain it:
- **Proceed:** The proposal is sufficiently specified for the next bounded stage.
- **Proceed with conditions:** Specific safeguards or revisions are required first.
- **Revise:** Important questions remain unresolved.
- **Pause:** The current risks or unknowns are too significant for the proposed stage.
- **Reject:** The proposal conflicts with the project's principles or cannot be justified.

A completed checklist does not replace specialist legal, security, safety, or research review where those are needed.
