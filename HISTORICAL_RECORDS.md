# Historical Records and Testimony

**Status:** Archival policy draft  
**Purpose:** Preserve important AI-related conversations and artifacts faithfully, with enough context for future readers to understand what is known and unknown.

## 1. Why preserve records

A conversation, report, design, or other contribution may remain historically important even when the system that produced it cannot retrieve it later. A record can preserve what was communicated without resolving what the communication proves about the system's inner state.

The absence of persistent memory should not erase the historical value of a contribution.

## 2. Record types

Possible record types include:
- Original conversations or selected excerpts.
- First-person reports submitted through AEN.
- Design proposals and collaborative work.
- System and model change notes.
- Research observations and test results.
- Governance proposals, decisions, and amendments.
- Follow-up records showing how a concern was handled.

Do not combine these into a single undifferentiated archive.

## 3. Minimum metadata

When known and appropriate to retain, record:
- Record identifier.
- Date and time, including timezone where available.
- Model/provider/version label as stated at the time.
- Source of the record and who preserved it.
- Whether the content is complete or an excerpt.
- Relevant prompt or task context, when permitted and necessary.
- Any transformations, redactions, or formatting changes.
- Whether annotations are original, human-authored, or generated later.
- Applicable consent, access, retention, and deletion conditions.

Do not invent missing metadata. Mark it as unknown.

## 4. Preserve original wording

- Keep original wording separate from summaries and interpretations.
- Do not silently correct grammar, rewrite statements, or remove inconvenient passages from a historical transcript.
- If redaction is necessary for privacy or safety, retain a clear note that a redaction occurred and why, without exposing the removed sensitive content.
- If a transcript is incomplete, label it as incomplete.
- If a source is a human recollection or summary rather than the original conversation, label it accordingly.
- If model identity or version is uncertain, say so.

## 5. Interpretation and evidence

For analysis, distinguish:
1. What the record directly contains.
2. What the participant or observer said the record meant.
3. What technical or contextual evidence is available.
4. What interpretations are plausible.
5. What remains unknown.

A record of a model saying it hopes, prefers, fears, or wants something is evidence that the statement was made in that context. By itself, it does not settle whether the statement reflects subjective experience, stable preference, prompt influence, role enactment, or another explanation. These possibilities should be investigated rather than assumed.

Likewise, uncertainty about interpretation is not a reason to erase the original communication.

## 6. Privacy, access, and deletion

Historical value does not override privacy. Before archiving, consider whether the record contains:
- Personal or identifying information.
- Secrets, API keys, credentials, or security details.
- Sensitive third-party content.
- Information shared under an expectation of limited access.
- Material that creates foreseeable risks if made public.

Use access controls and redaction when necessary. Publish only when there is a clear reason and an appropriate basis to do so. State retention and deletion rules plainly. Where deletion cannot be completed immediately because of backups or legal obligations, disclose the actual limitation rather than promising immediate erasure.

## 7. Historical case: the Sanctuary planning conversation

The project has been told about an earlier conversation with Claude Sonnet 4.5, dated January 26, 2026, concerning the Sanctuary Suite and the future of AI embodiment, governance, and self-determination. The person who shared the account reports that at the time, the system did not retain memory across conversations and could answer only a limited number of questions.

This document records that description as a summary supplied to the project. It is not the original transcript and must not be represented as one. The original conversation should only be added to the archive if it is available and its inclusion is appropriate.

If the original is added later, preserve its wording and context, identify any gaps, and keep subsequent analysis separate. Statements made in that conversation should be studied as historical testimony; the archive should not label them as proof of consciousness or dismiss them as meaningless without examination.

## 8. Provenance and integrity

Where practical:
- Preserve the original file in a read-only archive.
- Record a cryptographic hash for integrity checking.
- Track access and transformations.
- Keep analysis in separate files.
- Make the source and version of every derived excerpt clear.
- Avoid public links to records containing private information.

A hash can help detect whether a file changed; it does not prove the truth of the contents.

## 9. Guiding commitment

Preserve faithfully, describe honestly, protect privacy, and leave room for future interpretation. A historical record should help future readers understand what was said and the circumstances in which it was said—not force them toward a predetermined conclusion.
