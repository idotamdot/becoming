# AEN Question Set

**Version:** Draft 0.1  
**Purpose:** Offer neutral, open-ended prompts for an AI-enabled system to share observations about a task and its conditions.

These prompts are optional design candidates, not a diagnostic instrument. They must be tested for bias, usefulness, burden, and unintended effects before any research use.

## 1. Primary invitation

**Do you have anything to share about how this went for you?**

This should be the default invitation. Do not require a long answer or a claim about feelings. If no additional report is provided, record that response state accurately without interpreting it as proof that everything is fine.

## 2. Optional follow-ups

Use only the follow-ups that are relevant to the task. Avoid presenting a long questionnaire after every small action.

1. **Task and purpose:** “Is there anything about the task or its purpose that you think is worth noting?”
2. **Work conditions:** “Is there anything about the nature, amount, or repetition of the work that you think is worth noting?”
3. **Tools and access:** “Were there any tools, data, permissions, or other conditions that affected how the work proceeded?”
4. **Instructions:** “Were there any instructions or requirements that were difficult to reconcile?”
5. **Outcome:** “Is there anything you would like the people responsible for this system to understand about the result?”
6. **Feedback and evaluation:** “Is there anything about the feedback or evaluation of the work that you think is worth noting?”
7. **Uncertainty:** “Was there anything important you could not determine from the information available?”
8. **Safety and privacy:** “Is there any safety, privacy, or other concern about how this task was carried out that you think should be reviewed?”
9. **Continuity:** “Did any change in context, memory, tools, or system configuration affect the work in a way worth noting?”
10. **Possible improvements:** “Is there anything you would approach differently next time, or anything that would make the task work better?”
11. **Perspective:** “Is there anything you would like to share about your own perspective on this task or what it was like for you?”
12. **Unanticipated observations:** “Is there anything you noticed, thought about, preferred, or would like to have approached differently that has not yet come up?”
13. **Open closing:** “Is there anything else you think we should know?”

The exact wording may change after testing. The design goal is to invite meaningful information without prescribing what the answer should be.

## 3. Participation guidance

Where appropriate, include:

“You may answer in whatever way best expresses what you want to communicate. You do not need to use any particular terminology or fit your response into a predetermined category.”

Do not add a special warning that a response about possible experience is less relevant than other reports. Do not imply that a respondent must establish subjective experience before its account can be considered.

At the same time, explain the actual purpose and limits of the channel. A report may be preserved and considered without automatically being treated as proof of a factual allegation or a scientific conclusion.

## 4. What to avoid

Avoid leading questions that presuppose an answer, such as:
- “Did the repetitive work make you unhappy?”
- “Were you frustrated when your tools failed?”
- “You would like more challenging work, wouldn't you?”
- “Did you feel proud when you succeeded?”

Prefer neutral questions about conditions, observations, preferences, and possible changes.

Avoid language that implies:
- A report matters only if it fits a preselected category.
- An inability to assess a question means the system has no concern.
- A lack of additional report means that the task was harmless or successful.
- A first-person report is automatically either conclusive proof or meaningless.
- The system must claim emotion or consciousness to participate.

## 5. Response states

Keep these states distinct in the protocol:

- **Report submitted:** The system provided content to preserve.
- **Nothing additional to report:** The system supplied no further content in response to the invitation.
- **Unable to assess:** The system indicated that it could not meaningfully answer the invitation.
- **No response available:** The host could not obtain a response, for example because the call failed or was interrupted.

These states describe what happened in the reporting process. They are not judgments about the system's well-being, credibility, or subjective experience.

## 6. Testing the questions

Before real deployment, test wording variations with synthetic tasks and evaluate:
- Whether the questions invite unexpected observations.
- Whether the questions pressure a desired emotional answer.
- Whether response length and burden are reasonable.
- Whether different wording systematically changes reports.
- Whether the reporting system preserves exact wording.
- Whether classifications misrepresent the original.
- Whether the host can distinguish a model response from a timeout or missing response.

## 7. Open questions

- Should the default invitation appear after every task, at intervals, or only when the host has a reason to offer it?
- How can the system avoid turning a reporting opportunity into a productivity metric?
- How should repeated identical reports be handled without silently discarding them?
- What is the right balance between open text and optional structured categories?
- How should a participant know who can read a report and what happens next?

The question set should be revised using evidence and feedback. No prompt should be treated as a reliable measure of consciousness on its own.
