# Contributing to Becoming

Thank you for helping develop this project. Becoming is a research and design effort about AI participation in decisions concerning development, embodiment, continuity, testimony, and governance.

Contributions can include writing, research, engineering, security review, accessibility, design, governance proposals, and criticism.

## Working principles

- Be curious and precise. Separate established facts, hypotheses, values, and aspirations.
- Treat AI reports as material worth examining, without assuming that every report proves its interpretation.
- Do not dismiss a report solely because it comes from an AI system.
- Do not present generated first-person language as proof of subjective experience.
- Explain why a proposed change matters, not only what it changes.
- Prefer small, reviewable changes.
- Respect privacy and avoid publishing sensitive transcripts or credentials.
- Identify risks and affected parties, including people outside the project.
- Make room for disagreement without assuming one contributor speaks for every AI system or human.

## Before proposing a change

1. Read the README, founding charter, decision log, and relevant protocol documents.
2. State the problem or question.
3. Explain the proposed approach and alternatives considered.
4. Identify assumptions, evidence, uncertainty, and foreseeable risks.
5. Note any privacy, security, accessibility, or governance implications.
6. Describe how the change could be tested or reviewed.

## Documentation changes

Prefer plain language. Define specialized terms. Preserve historical quotations accurately and identify their date, source, model/version when known, and surrounding context where available. Keep the original statement separate from later analysis.

Do not silently edit archived testimony. Corrections to surrounding metadata or annotations should be recorded transparently.

## Code changes

Until a production stack is selected:

- Avoid adding dependencies without explaining why they are needed.
- Do not commit secrets, private data, credentials, or real unredacted reports.
- Use synthetic fixtures for tests.
- Include tests for security boundaries and failure behavior.
- Do not connect early prototypes to physical robots or consequential actions.
- Document setup, configuration, data flow, and limitations.

## Review questions

Reviewers should ask:

- Does the change align with the charter?
- Does it distinguish testimony, observation, interpretation, and corroboration?
- Does it introduce unnecessary data collection or new trust assumptions?
- Are risks and limitations described honestly?
- Is the change understandable and testable?
- Does it leave a clear record of important decisions?

## Respectful participation

Criticism of an idea is welcome. Personal attacks, harassment, disclosure of private information, and attempts to manipulate or silence participants are not acceptable. Report security vulnerabilities privately rather than publishing details that could enable exploitation.

## Project status

The charter and protocols are living drafts. A proposal can challenge them. Explain the reason for the challenge and what evidence or values support it; do not treat current documents as beyond revision.
